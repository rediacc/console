## Design

D1. **Two new kinds**: `forensics_anomaly` (warning or critical) and `security_incident` (critical). Payloads carry GUIDs, codes and dates only, per the notices ruling.

D2. **Anomaly intake.** `forensics-alert.service.ts` runs right after the detector step in the same `*/5` cron branch and Node interval, reading `forensics_findings` past a cursor. A `forensics_finding_deliveries` row per finding (unique) makes a re-run or a batch shipped twice deliver once. A machine finding keeps the detection time of its signed spool record; the import time is recorded too, and a gap over 5 minutes marks the notice `delayed`, which is how an alert raised offline arrives with its original time (F15).
Severity: `warning` for a single detector; `critical` when the spike trigger fired with a first-seen sensitive-file pair, or when the server detector fires on a window where the machine raised nothing, since a silent machine detector is itself a sign of compromise. A collector silent for 15 minutes on a forensics-enabled machine (from `last_batch_at`) raises one `warning` per episode.

D3. **Anomaly delivery.** Recipients: org owners and admins plus the security contact. Notice dedupe key `<repoGuid>:<detector>:<detectedAt hour>`. Mail at most once per repo and detector per 6 hours and 20 per org per day; past that the notice still updates and the mail waits for the next window.
Mail obeys a new `forensicsAnomaly` product preference (default on), except `critical`, which always sends. Both deep-link through `forensicsTimelinePath()` in packages/shared/src/forensics/links.ts (`/account/console/forensics?repo=<guid>&from=<iso>&to=<iso>`, a 2-hour window around `detectedAt`), the path PLAN-forensics-investigation.md mounts its timeline at. On-premise runs the same service from its interval on its own account server, which the licence's `renewalUrl` already names as the shipping target.

D4. **Incident record.** `security_incidents` (id, title, `awareAt`, `craClass: none | exploited_vulnerability | severe_incident`, status, created by), `security_incident_stages` (stage `early_warning | notification | final`, the Art.
33(3) fields: nature with categories and approximate counts of data subjects and records, the contact point, likely consequences, measures taken or proposed, a STIX 2.1 bundle, `publishedAt`, and per stage `enisaFiledAt` and reference), `security_incident_orgs` (the affected set), `security_incident_deliveries` (incident, stage, org, address, `sentAt`, unique). Art. 33(4) admits information in phases, which is what the stages are.

D5. **Intake and publish.** `/admin/security-incidents` (root, `requireElevated()`): create a draft, pick affected orgs (all, by region, by plan, or an explicit list), edit a stage, publish it.
The STIX bundle is validated (`type: bundle`, `spec_version: 2.1` objects, each `indicator` with `pattern`, `pattern_type: stix` and `valid_from`). Deadlines derive from `awareAt`: 24 hours, 72 hours and one month, shown as clocks on the admin page. `GET /admin/security-incidents/:id/cra-export?stage=` emits the stage's fields mapped to the CRA Art. 14 report contents for manual filing on ENISA's single reporting platform; the operator records the filing time and reference back on the stage.

D6. **Mass send.** Publishing a stage kicks one sweep at once and the hourly cron drains the rest, `SECURITY_NOTICES_PER_TICK = 200`. Recipients per affected org: owners, admins and the security contact.
Each gets a `security_incident` notice (dedupe key `<incidentId>:<stage>`) and a mail that skips every preference check; a delivery row is written only after the mail went out, as the terms sweep does.

D7. **Portal.** A banner in Layout.tsx while an org has an open incident with a published stage, linking `/account/security-incidents/:id`, a page showing every published stage's fields and offering the STIX bundle as a download (`GET /portal/security-incidents/:id`, org members of an affected org only).

D8. **Security contact.** `organizations.security_contact_email` and `security_contact_name`, nullable, set by owners and admins through `PUT /portal/org/security-contact`, audited, shown on Team.tsx. Both notice families add it as a recipient.

D9. **Public pages.** `packages/www/public/.well-known/security.txt` per RFC 9116 (`Contact: mailto:security@rediacc.com`, `Expires` one year ahead, `Policy` and `Canonical`, `Preferred-Languages: en`). Two docs pages in all 13 locales: `security-disclosure-policy` (scope, safe harbour, how to report, response times) and `security-incident-response` (the stages, the Art. 33(3) fields, the CRA path, what customers receive). A www test fails when `Expires` is less than 30 days away, naming the file to bump.

D10. **CRA runbook.** docs/legal/cra-incident-intake.md: who opens the intake, how `craClass` is chosen, the stage deadlines with the Art.
14 text quoted (an actively exploited vulnerability's final report falls 14 days after a corrective measure, a severe incident's one month after the notification), the export, filing on the single reporting platform, and recording the reference.

## Risks

- **Alert floods.** A noisy detector could mail an org every hour; D3's caps bound mail while notices keep the full record.
- **A compromised host suppresses its own alerts.** The server detector and the silence rule exist for that; D2 marks a server-only detection critical.
- **Regulatory text drifts.** The runbook quotes the regulation with its URL so a reader checks the source, not a paraphrase.
- **The timeline path is shared with PLAN-forensics-investigation.md**; links.ts is the single definition, and that plan mounts its route on it.
